01
About me

It started with my own spine

I grew up with scoliosis. It quietly took things from me — swimming, sports, the ordinary physical stuff my friends never had to think about — and it put me in chronic pain most days.

The curve showed in how I stood, and kids noticed. Being made fun of for the way my own back held me up is a particular kind of hurt, and it's the reason none of this work is abstract to me.

02
Why I build

Got Your Back

I've been seeing a chiropractor for over ten years. In that time I learned my own back — which positions cost me, which exercises give something back, how much of the pain is correctable before it becomes permanent.

Got Your Back came out of that. I'm still building it through college, and still adding to the app around it, because the version of me who didn't know any of this deserved a device that noticed before the pain did.

03
The other half

What the gym gave back

I used to be overweight, and I heard about it — fat lard and worse. Those names sat with me for years longer than the people who said them did.

The gym is where I got to answer them. It's now one of the few places my back and my head both feel good, which is exactly why the health tools I build keep pointing there.

04
Currently

Building in stealth

I'm helping develop a product at a stealth-stage company. I can't say much about it yet, but the shape is this: one place to log your nutrition and your lifts, so effort in the gym and effort in the kitchen finally read as one picture.

I tested the AI nutrition scanner on myself before asking anyone else to trust it — down 7 lbs, lower body fat, strength still climbing. I only ship health tools I've been the first user of.
